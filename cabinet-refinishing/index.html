In short

The Painter Hotline cabinet crew refinishes kitchen and bathroom cabinets across Colorado. Cabinets are degreased, scuff-sanded, primed with a bonding primer formulated for slick factory finishes and sprayed with a cabinet-grade topcoat. Doors and drawer fronts are sprayed in a controlled area while boxes are finished in place under containment. Published Front Range data puts cabinet refinishing at roughly $2,000 to $8,000 per kitchen against $15,000 to $30,000 or more for replacement.

Four steps, and every chipped cabinet job skipped one

  1. Degrease. Cabinets near a cooktop carry years of aerosolized cooking residue. No primer bonds through grease. This step is boring and non-negotiable.
  2. Scuff sand. Factory finishes are deliberately slick so food wipes off. That same slickness rejects coatings. Sanding creates tooth.
  3. Bonding primer. Not ordinary primer. A product formulated specifically to grip a cured factory finish.
  4. Cabinet-grade topcoat. Wall paint never cures hard enough to survive fingernails and doors closing a hundred times a week.

Every failed cabinet job we are called in to fix skipped at least one of those, usually two. The failure shows up at the edges within about a year, and correcting it costs more than doing it right would have.

Why sprayed, and why that needs a crew

You can identify a brushed cabinet door from across a room: brush tracks, paint pooling at the panel edges, a surface that never reads as furniture. Spraying lays the coating flat and even, which is the entire point of refinishing rather than replacing.

Spraying well is a practiced skill. Gun setup, product viscosity, pass overlap and flash time between coats all matter, and a crew that sprays occasionally gets occasional results. Ours sprays daily.

The oak grain decision

Colorado has a tremendous amount of golden and honey oak from the 1980s and 90s. Oak has deep open grain and paint does not fill it. Two honest options: leave the grain visible, which reads as painted wood and keeps character, or fill it for a smooth modern slab look, which adds labor. We will show you both on your own doors before you choose.

The countertop detail nobody mentions

Replacing cabinets almost always drags countertop replacement with it, because old counters rarely survive removal and almost never fit new boxes. That can add several thousand dollars a replacement quote does not always make obvious. Refinishing leaves your counters exactly where they are.

Questions

Cabinet questions

How much does cabinet refinishing cost?

Published Front Range data puts professional cabinet refinishing at roughly $2,000 to $8,000 for a typical kitchen. Door and drawer-front count, whether boxes are included, and whether you want the oak grain filled drive the number.

Is refinishing cheaper than replacing?

Substantially. Replacement commonly starts near $15,000 and runs past $30,000 for custom work, and it usually brings countertop replacement along with it. Refinishing leaves your counters in place.

Can honey oak cabinets be painted white?

Yes, and it is the most requested cabinet project we get. The question to settle first is whether you want the oak grain visible or filled smooth. We will show you both on your own doors.

Do you spray or brush?

Spray, always. Doors and drawer fronts are finished in a controlled area and boxes are sprayed in place behind containment. Brushed cabinets look brushed, which defeats the purpose.

How long is my kitchen out of action?

Three to five days on site. You keep countertops and appliances throughout. Doors are off for most of it, so plan on open shelving for the week.

Can you fix a cabinet job someone else did badly?

Usually. We assess whether the existing coating can be sanded and recoated or has to come back to bare, then rebuild the finish properly.
