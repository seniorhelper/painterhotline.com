Is Painter Hotline the right company for your project?

Step 1 of 7

Before we talk about paint, one question.

Have you ever hired a contractor and regretted it? Not necessarily a painter. Anyone.

We get called out to fix other painters' work more than you would think.

Not occasionally. Regularly. Enough that we have a page about it.

And almost none of those jobs failed because somebody picked a bad color or rolled it badly. They failed for one of four reasons, every time:

What was skippedHow it shows upWhen
The washPaint sitting on chalk and dust lets go in sheets12 to 24 months
Scraping to a sound edgeThe old failing layer takes the new one with itFirst hard winter
Primer on bare or repaired substrateBlistering and peeling, usually low on the wallWithin two seasons
Caulking the jointsWater gets behind the film and freeze-thaw lifts itOne to three winters

None of those are visible on the day the crew drives away. That is exactly the problem: a bad paint job and a good one look identical for about eighteen months.

We are not the cheapest bid you will get. We would rather tell you that now than have you find out in year two.

We do not send the same three guys at everything.

Most painting companies have one crew that does whatever comes in. Cabinets on Monday, a warehouse on Wednesday, your living room on Friday. Those are genuinely different trades.

We run separate crews, and they stay in their lane:

InteriorExteriorCabinets CommercialDecks & fencesSpecialty coatings

The cabinet crew sprays every day, so your doors come back looking like furniture rather than brushwork. The commercial crew works nights and phases a building so nobody loses a day of trade. The exterior crew knows what Colorado altitude and chinook winds do to a coating, and sequences the elevations around it.

That is the actual answer to "why would yours be different." Not a slogan. A different staffing model.

We are not perfect, and we will not pretend otherwise.

On a long enough timeline, every painting company has a day that goes sideways. A board that was worse underneath than it looked. A color that reads differently on the wall than on the chip. Weather that moves a schedule.

What we will promise is narrower and more useful:

  • We show up. If a day moves, you hear it from us rather than noticing an empty driveway.
  • We answer the phone. During the project and after the invoice. Same number either way.
  • We stand behind the work. Five years on workmanship, in writing, with the exclusions actually printed instead of buried.
  • We tell you the hard thing early. If your siding needs carpentry first, you hear it at the estimate, not after you have paid for coatings that were going to fail.

Attention to detail and the kind of customer service we would want in our own homes. That is in the mission, and it is the part we actually get judged on.

People first, then profits.

That order matters and it is not decoration. A painting company that optimizes for margin first starts skipping the wash, thinning the coats and quoting "prep as needed." It works for about two years.

We would rather be the company you call the next time something needs coating, and the one you give your neighbor's number to. That only happens if the first project was handled properly and somebody picked up when you called afterward.

Family-friendly, straightforward, and still answering in year four. That is the whole business model.

About the walkthrough

How long does the walkthrough take?

About two minutes. Seven short steps covering how we are organized, why most paint jobs fail, what repainting twice actually costs, and what we will and will not promise. It ends with a single yes or no question about whether we can look at your project.

Do I have to give my details to go through it?

No. Nothing is required until the final step, and only if you answer yes. You can read the whole thing and leave.

What happens if I say yes?

A short form appears for your contact details, the type of work and photos. A written estimate comes back within one business day, naming every surface, preparation step, product, sheen, coat count and the warranty.

What happens if I say no?

Nothing. No follow-up calls. You get our number to save and the four questions worth asking any painter, including whoever you do end up hiring.

Is this just a sales pitch?

It is a sales page, and we would rather say so than pretend. It is also accurate: the failure modes, the arithmetic and the crew structure are all real, and you can verify them against any other bid you get.
