In short

The Painter Hotline exterior crew paints siding, trim, fascia, soffit, doors and garage doors across Colorado. Every exterior begins with washing, scraping to a sound edge, sanding transitions, filling and caulking, and priming bare or repaired substrate before any finish coat. Colorado altitude raises ultraviolet intensity, downslope chinook winds dry coatings faster than manufacturers intend, and freeze-thaw cycling works every open joint, so elevations are sequenced by exposure and product grade is matched to the load.

Four forces, and all four compound here

Exterior paint does not simply wear out. It is taken apart by identifiable forces, and in Colorado they stack.

  1. Ultraviolet at altitude. Thinner atmosphere filters less. UV attacks the binder holding pigment together, which is what produces fading first and chalking later. Rub a sunny wall: if powder comes off, that coating is finished.
  2. Chinook winds. Downslope winds accelerate off the foothills, skin a surface before the film underneath has knitted, and drive grit into it.
  3. Freeze-thaw. Water entering a failed caulk joint freezes, expands and lifts the film from the substrate. Dozens of cycles a winter. This is why caulking is structural work rather than cosmetic.
  4. Snow reflection. Fresh snow bounces ultraviolet back upward onto lower walls, trim and the underside of eaves. Those areas routinely fail before anything above them.

The season, honestly

The practical exterior window runs mid-May to early October. Denver-area climate records put the average last spring freeze near May 5 and the average first fall freeze near October 7, and elevation tightens both ends. The governing number is the overnight low, not the daytime high, because a coating applied at a pleasant 65 degrees still has to cure through the night. If it drops below the product's minimum before the film knits, adhesion suffers in ways that do not show for a year.

We will move a date rather than paint into a bad window. That is occasionally inconvenient and it is the correct call every time.

What is in the scope

  1. Protect plants, walks, drives, windows, fixtures and vehicles.
  2. Pressure wash the whole exterior and allow full dry time.
  3. Scrape every loose edge back to sound material.
  4. Repair or replace failed substrate, and seal all cut ends.
  5. Sand transitions so the old paint line does not telegraph through.
  6. Fill, then caulk every joint, seam and penetration.
  7. Prime all bare substrate and every repair.
  8. Two finish coats on siding, trim, fascia, soffit and doors as scoped.
  9. Full cleanup, then a walkthrough you lead before the balance is due.

Questions

Exterior questions

When can you paint the outside of my house?

Generally mid-May through early October. Denver-area records place the average last spring freeze near May 5 and the first fall freeze near October 7, and elevation tightens both ends. The overnight low governs, because the coating cures through the night.

How much does exterior painting cost?

Published 2026 Front Range data puts exterior house painting at roughly $1.55 to $4.10 per square foot of paintable surface. The condition of the existing coating and any substrate repair move the number far more than square footage does.

Do you pressure wash first?

Always, on every exterior. Paint will not bond to chalk, dust or mildew, and Colorado's sun-facing walls chalk heavily. Washing is part of the scope, not an add-on.

How many coats do you apply?

Two finish coats on siding and trim as standard, with primer specified separately wherever the substrate is bare or repaired. The count is written into your estimate rather than left vague.

Why does my west wall look worse than the rest?

Directional ultraviolet load. West and south elevations take hours of direct afternoon sun, which breaks down the binder in the coating. Those walls commonly need attention a full cycle before north and east.

Can I repaint only part of the exterior?

Yes, and it is often the smart use of a limited budget. Doing the sun-facing elevations now and the shaded ones in a later season matches the spend to the actual wear.
