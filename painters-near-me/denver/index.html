Local conditions

What painting in Denver involves

Brick is the default, and it is a decision

Denver built in brick for most of its history, so a large share of the houses you see are masonry with painted wood trim, porches and soffits. Painting brick that has never been painted is effectively permanent, and it needs a breathable masonry coating rather than house paint. Most Denver exteriors we price are really trim, porch and window jobs wrapped around brick.

Old houses, old paint

More than a third of Denver homes were built before 1960. Porch ceilings, window sashes and stair trim in those houses commonly carry several generations of coatings, and the bottom layers are the ones most likely to contain lead. Scraping them is controlled work, not a quick pass with a heat gun.

Alleys, tight lots and street parking

Many older neighborhoods have detached garages on the alley, five-foot side setbacks and no driveway. Ladders, drop cloths, containment and a place to park the trailer get planned before the first day, and neighbors get a heads-up when a side yard is shared.

Newer Denver is a different job

Stapleton-era Central Park, Green Valley Ranch and the infill rowhomes around the light rail are mostly fiber cement and engineered trim on their first or second repaint. There the work is caulk replacement, sun-faded south and west walls, and following any community color rules.

How old Denver's homes are

Questions

Denver painting questions

Do you paint brick houses in Denver?

We paint the wood on them every week: trim, porches, eaves and windows. Painting the brick itself is a bigger decision because it cannot really be undone. If the brick is already painted, we clean, repair failing areas and recoat with a breathable masonry product. If it has never been painted, we talk through limewash, stain or leaving it alone before we quote paint.

Can you work on a Denver home with an alley garage and no driveway?

Yes. We plan staging, parking and access before the start date, and we fold the garage into the same mobilization if you want it painted too.

How many Denver homes might have lead paint?

By the Census count, 57.4% of homes in Denver predate 1980, and any of those could hide lead paint (banned for homes in 1978). Tell us your year built.
