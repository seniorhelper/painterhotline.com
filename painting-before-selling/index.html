Painting before you sell: what is worth doing, and what is not.

Paint is one of the few pre-listing projects that changes both the photos and the inspection report. The trick is spending on the right surfaces, in the right order, before the photographer arrives.

Painting before selling works best when it targets what buyers and inspectors notice: peeling or failed exterior paint, water and smoke stains, scuffed trim and doors, patched walls, and rooms in colors that photograph badly. In Colorado, timing also matters, because exterior paint has a season, while interiors can be done year-round. Homes built before 1978 bring federal lead-paint disclosure rules for sellers and lead-safe work rules for painters. What buyers and inspectors notice first

Most buyers first see your home in listing photos, usually on a phone. Paint problems that you have stopped noticing are obvious there: scuffed hallways, a patched wall in a slightly different white, a water stain on a ceiling, a bold accent wall, trim that has yellowed. Then an inspector walks the house, and peeling exterior paint or exposed wood reads as deferred maintenance, which tends to prompt questions about what else was deferred.

Painting is not about hiding problems. A water stain should be traced and the cause fixed before it is primed and painted, and that repair belongs in your disclosures if your agent says so. The goal is to stop cosmetic wear from distracting buyers from the house itself.

What to paint, in order

  1. Failed exterior paint and exposed wood. Peeling, flaking or bare trim and siding is the item most likely to come back to you as an inspection request. Buyers using FHA or VA financing can also run into appraisal conditions on chipping or peeling paint, especially on older homes, so ask your agent or lender early if that applies to likely buyers.
  2. Stains. Water, smoke and tannin stains bleed through ordinary paint. They need a stain-blocking primer first, after the cause is fixed.
  3. High-traffic walls, trim and doors. Hallways, stairwells, entries and kids' rooms show wear fastest and photograph worst.
  4. The front door and entry. Small area, big effect, and it is the first thing a buyer touches.
  5. Strong or dated colors. Dark, saturated or very personal colors take extra coats to cover, but they are often the rooms that look worst in photos.
  6. Kitchen cabinets, sometimes. Refinishing tired cabinets can change the look of a kitchen for much less than replacing them, but it takes longer than walls. Our cabinet crew can tell you whether your timeline allows it.

Colors that sell well

  • One calm palette through the main living areas. A consistent warm white, light greige or soft neutral makes rooms read as connected and larger in photos.
  • Bright white, flat ceilings to bounce light and hide imperfections.
  • Sheen by room: matte or eggshell on walls, satin or semi-gloss on trim, doors, kitchens and baths where it will be wiped.
  • Test in Colorado light. Strong, high-altitude sun washes colors out on south and west walls and shifts them through the day. Look at samples on the actual wall in the morning and late afternoon before deciding.
  • Check your HOA before changing exterior colors. Many Colorado HOAs require approval of exterior colors in advance, and that approval can take time.

Timing around Colorado's seasons

Interiors can be painted any time of year. Exterior paint needs dry surfaces and temperatures within the manufacturer's limits, day and night. On the Front Range that usually means late spring through early fall; the National Weather Service puts Denver's average last spring freeze in early May and first fall freeze in early October. If you are listing in winter, do the interior now and agree with your agent how to handle exterior touch-ups, or plan exterior work for the first good weeks of spring.

Work backward from your photo date, not your listing date. Leave a few days after painting before staging: most latex paints dry to the touch quickly but take considerably longer to fully cure, and furniture pushed against fresh walls can mark them.

Homes built before 1978: two rules to know

For you as the seller: under the federal Lead Disclosure Rule, sellers of most housing built before 1978 must disclose any known lead-based paint or hazards, give buyers the EPA pamphlet Protect Your Family From Lead in Your Home, include a lead warning statement in the contract, and give buyers a 10-day opportunity for a lead inspection or risk assessment, which buyers may waive. Your agent will have the forms.

For whoever paints: paid work that disturbs paint in a pre-1978 home falls under the EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting rule, which requires lead-safe work practices: containment, specific cleanup and verification. Ask any painter you hire about it; our older homes guide lists what to ask. That is why our quote form asks for the year your home was built.

What moves the price

We do not publish a flat pre-listing price because every house is different. The factors that move a quote are:

  • Number and size of rooms, and whether ceilings, trim and doors are included.
  • Wall condition: patching, nail holes, stains needing primer.
  • Color change: going from dark to light takes more coats.
  • Vacant or occupied: empty homes are faster to paint.
  • Exterior height, condition and season.
  • How tight your deadline is.

Questions

Painting before selling: questions

Is it worth painting a house before selling it?

Often, when the paint is worn, stained, peeling or in strong colors. Paint changes how the home looks in listing photos and removes easy inspection items. Fresh paint over sound, neutral walls in good condition adds less.

What color should I paint my house before selling?

A consistent, calm neutral through the main living areas, bright white flat ceilings, and durable satin or semi-gloss trim. Test samples on the actual walls, because strong Colorado sun shifts colors through the day. Check HOA rules before changing exterior colors.

Should I paint the exterior before listing?

Fix failed, peeling paint and exposed wood, because inspectors and some lenders notice it. A full exterior repaint depends on condition, season and budget. Exterior paint needs suitable temperatures, so in Colorado that work is usually done from late spring to early fall.

My house was built before 1978. What do I need to know?

Sellers of most pre-1978 housing must disclose known lead-based paint, provide the EPA pamphlet, include a lead warning statement and offer buyers a 10-day inspection opportunity. Paid painting that disturbs old paint must follow the EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting rule's lead-safe practices.

How quickly can a house be painted before listing?

It depends on size, condition, color changes and whether the home is vacant. Send photos and your listing date through the virtual estimate and we will tell you what fits the timeline.

Should I paint over a water stain before selling?

Fix the cause first and ask your agent whether it needs to be disclosed. Then the stain needs a stain-blocking primer before paint, or it will bleed through.
