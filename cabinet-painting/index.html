In the scope

Why sprayed cabinets look different from brushed ones

  • Degreasing. Invisible kitchen grease is the number one cause of cabinet finish failure, so every surface is cleaned first.
  • Scuff sanding. Factory finishes are slick, and sanding gives primer something to grip.
  • Bonding primer. Formulated to stick to cured finishes, laminate and thermofoil.
  • Sprayed coats. Cabinet-grade finish applied with spray equipment, so no brush marks or roller texture.
  • Controlled drying. Doors finished where air is clean, not leaning against a dusty garage wall.
  • Containment. Boxes finished in place with the rest of the house protected from overspray.
  • Hardware handled. Removed, labeled and reinstalled, or new hardware fitted if you are upgrading.

Start to finish

How a cabinet project runs

  1. Count the pieces

    Doors, drawer fronts and boxes counted, existing finish checked, color and sheen confirmed.

  2. Remove and label

    Doors, drawers and hardware come off and are labeled to return to the exact opening.

  3. Clean, sand, prime

    Degreasing, scuff sanding, filling and bonding primer on every surface.

  4. Spray the finish

    Multiple thin coats with proper cure time between them.

  5. Reinstall and inspect

    Doors rehung, hardware set, hinges adjusted, then a walkthrough with you.

Straight answers

Cabinet painting questions

How much does cabinet painting cost?

Professional cabinet refinishing commonly runs about $2,000 to $8,000 per kitchen. The real driver is the number of doors, drawer fronts and boxes, plus the condition of the existing finish.

Is painting cheaper than replacing?

Substantially. Replacement is commonly quoted from around $15,000 into the $30,000s, while refinishing typically runs a fraction of that when the boxes are solid.

How long does it take?

Most kitchens take three to five days on site, driven by cure time between coats.

Will the finish chip?

Not when the process is right. Chipping traces back to skipped degreasing, no scuff sanding, missing bonding primer or wall paint used instead of a cabinet-grade product.

Can you fix cabinets another painter did badly?

Usually. We assess whether the existing coating can be sanded and recoated or has to come off, then rebuild the finish properly.

Can I use the kitchen during the project?

Mostly. Boxes are finished in place under containment, so there are periods when the kitchen is off limits, and we plan that schedule with you.

Can you paint laminate or thermofoil?

Often, with the right bonding primer. Thermofoil peeling from the substrate is a repair question first.

Do you paint vanities and built-ins?

Yes. Vanities, bookcases, entertainment centers, mudroom lockers and laundry cabinetry use the same process.
