How to know your deck needs attention

Pour a cup of water on the boards. If it soaks in instead of beading, the seal is gone and the wood is drinking every storm.

Water soaks in

The clearest sign the finish has failed and the wood is absorbing moisture.

Gray, dull boards

UV has broken down surface fibers. Brightening restores tone before stain goes on.

Splinters and raised grain

Dry, weathered wood roughens. Sanding makes it safe for bare feet again.

Cracking or cupping

Repeated wet-dry cycles distort boards, and sealing slows it significantly.

Stain peeling in sheets

A film-forming product failed, usually from moisture underneath or poor prep.

Green or black patches

Shaded, damp areas and sprinkler spray grow mildew that needs cleaning, not covering.

What a proper deck project includes

  • Inspection first. Loose boards, popped fasteners and rot get flagged before stain is ever quoted.
  • Cleaning. Dirt, mildew, pollen and failing finish removed with the right products and technique.
  • Brightening. Restores natural tone and opens the grain so stain penetrates evenly.
  • Sanding where needed. Walking surfaces and rails smoothed for comfort and appearance.
  • Proper dry time. Wood must be dry before stain goes on, which is why weather drives the schedule.
  • Stain matched to the wood. Transparent, semi-transparent and solid options, with the trade-offs explained.
  • Everything matching. Rails, fences, pergolas and gates finished so the yard reads as one space.

Start to finish

How a deck or fence project runs

  1. Measure and inspect

    Deck surface, railings and fence runs measured, wood condition assessed.

  2. Clean and brighten

    Failing finish, mildew and gray weathering removed, tone restored.

  3. Dry time

    Wood dries to the product requirement. Staining damp wood is the fastest route to peeling.

  4. Sand and protect

    Rough areas smoothed, fasteners set, surrounding surfaces masked.

  5. Stain and inspect

    Applied evenly and worked into the grain with transitions planned to avoid lap lines.

Straight answers

Deck and fence questions

How often should a deck be stained in Colorado?

It depends on exposure and product. Test with water: when it stops beading and starts soaking in, it is time. South and west-facing decks need attention sooner, and mountain decks sooner still.

What is the best season for deck work?

Spring and early fall are usually ideal because moderate temperatures let stain absorb properly. The wood must be dry, and hot midday sun can cause blotching.

Why did my stain peel?

Usually trapped moisture, wood that was not clean or dry enough, or a film-forming product applied where water could get underneath it.

Can you stain a brand-new deck?

Often, though new pressure-treated lumber may need to weather and dry first. We check moisture rather than guessing.

Transparent, semi-transparent or solid?

Transparent shows the most grain and needs refreshing soonest; semi-transparent balances both; solid lasts longest in appearance but is hardest to reverse.

Do you stain fences and pergolas?

Yes, including privacy fences, railings, gates and planters, matched to the deck.

Can you repair boards first?

We flag loose boards, popped fasteners and rot. Minor fixes are handled in the project and larger repairs are quoted or referred.
