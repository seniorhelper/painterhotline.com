Colorado conditions

Paint ages faster here, and the elevation is only part of it

  • More ultraviolet light. The World Health Organization puts the increase at roughly 10% per 1,000 meters of elevation. Denver sits near 1,600 meters and Breckenridge near 2,900.
  • A long freeze-thaw season. National Weather Service records for Denver show freezes as early as September 8 and as late as June 8, and mountain towns stretch that further.
  • Hard daily swings. Siding expands and contracts constantly, so flexible caulk and a properly bonded film matter more here than almost anywhere.
  • Wind and grit. Open ground from Erie to Bennett carries dust onto wet film, which changes when and how a crew sprays.
  • Hail. Front Range storms chip trim and garage doors, and every chip is a way in for water.

The call we take most

"Somebody painted it two summers ago. Look at it now."

A serious share of our year is spent correcting work that failed early. Peeling trim, chalky siding, cabinet doors chipping at the edge. In Colorado, skipped preparation shows up fast, and the homeowner pays twice.

  • We find the cause first. Moisture, contamination, incompatible layers or missing primer. The fix is different for each.
  • We remove what is failing. Painting over loose paint buys a season, not a solution.
  • We correct the source. Sprinklers on siding, open caulk joints, failed gutters, bare end grain.
  • We put the warranty in writing. Five years, because the prep is real.
