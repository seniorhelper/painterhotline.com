What Does Residential Painting Actually Include?

Residential painting covers a wider range of surfaces and project types than many homeowners initially realize. At its core, it includes interior wall and ceiling painting, exterior siding and trim painting, and specialty surfaces like doors, shutters, and garage floors. It also extends to cabinet refinishing — one of the most transformative and cost-effective home improvement projects available — and to outdoor wood surfaces like decks, fences, and pergolas that need staining or sealing to survive Colorado's climate.

Every surface in and around your home that gets painted or stained falls under the residential painting umbrella. Therefore, it helps to work with a single trusted contractor who can handle everything consistently, rather than coordinating multiple specialists who may not communicate or match their work to each other's standards.

Interior Painting — Getting It Right the First Time

Interior painting seems straightforward, but the difference between a mediocre job and an exceptional one comes down almost entirely to preparation and technique. Paint applied to an improperly prepared surface will not adhere correctly. It will look uneven. It will chip at edges. It will fail in high-moisture areas like bathrooms and kitchens far sooner than it should.

Our interior painting process always begins with a thorough cleaning of all surfaces to be painted. Next, we fill any nail holes, dents, cracks, or surface imperfections with the appropriate filler and sand them smooth. We apply painter's tape with precision along all trim lines, ceiling edges, and fixture borders. We protect floors with canvas drop cloths and cover any furniture remaining in the room. Only then do we begin applying paint — starting with primer where needed, followed by the appropriate number of finish coats for the color and surface type involved.

Choosing the right sheen for each room matters more than most homeowners realize. Flat and matte finishes absorb light and hide surface imperfections beautifully — they work well in bedrooms, formal dining rooms, and other low-traffic areas. Eggshell is the most versatile choice for living rooms and hallways. Satin holds up better to cleaning and moisture, making it ideal for kitchens, bathrooms, and children's rooms. Semi-gloss and high-gloss are best reserved for trim, baseboards, doors, and cabinetry where durability and a distinct finish are the goal.

Exterior Painting — Built for Colorado's Climate

Exterior painting in Denver requires a different set of considerations than in most other parts of the country. The Mile High City's combination of intense UV radiation at altitude, 300-plus days of sunshine annually, dramatic freeze-thaw temperature cycles, and periodic hailstorms creates an environment that challenges every exterior paint system. Using the wrong product — or skipping proper prep — results in paint failures that cost significantly more to remediate than the original savings justified.

We start every exterior project with a thorough pressure wash to remove surface contaminants, mildew, and chalk from aging paint. After the surface dries completely, we scrape any loose or peeling areas and sand the edges smooth to create seamless transitions. We caulk every gap around windows, doors, trim boards, and utility penetrations. Water infiltration at these gaps is the leading cause of wood rot and paint failure on Denver homes. Caulking is not optional — it is essential.

For exterior surfaces, we work exclusively with 100% acrylic latex paints formulated for high-UV environments. Products like Sherwin-Williams Duration Exterior, Behr Marquee Exterior, and Benjamin Moore Aura Exterior represent the top tier of residential exterior paint performance. They cost more per gallon than budget alternatives. They also last two to four years longer in Colorado conditions, producing significantly better value over the lifespan of your paint job.

Cabinet Painting — The Smartest Kitchen Investment

Full kitchen cabinet replacement is one of the most expensive home improvement projects a Denver homeowner can undertake. Depending on materials, layout, and finishes, costs routinely range from $15,000 to $30,000 or more. Professional cabinet painting typically costs between $1,200 and $4,500 for a standard kitchen — and produces a finish quality that rivals new factory-finished cabinetry when done correctly.

The critical word is "correctly." Cabinet painting done improperly — with inadequate degreasing, wrong primer selection, or product applied in thick coats rather than multiple thin ones — produces a finish that chips, peels, and looks worse than the original within a year. Our cabinet painting process involves removing all doors and hardware, cleaning every surface with a professional degreaser, sanding to create the right surface profile, applying a bonding primer, and finishing with a durable alkyd or waterborne topcoat in multiple thin coats with proper cure time between applications. The result is a factory-smooth finish that lasts.

Deck Staining — Protecting Your Outdoor Investment

Colorado homeowners spend significant money on outdoor living spaces — decks, pergolas, fences, and privacy screens. Protecting those investments from the state's UV intensity, freeze-thaw cycles, and occasional heavy moisture requires the right product applied correctly at the right intervals. Most wood decks in Colorado need restaining every two to four years to maintain protection. Wait too long, and you risk surface checking, graying, and eventual moisture infiltration that leads to rot.

We use penetrating oil-based stains from TWP (Total Wood Preservative) and Armstrong Clark — two of the most respected deck stain brands in the industry. These products absorb into the wood fiber rather than forming a film on the surface, which means they flex with the wood's natural expansion and contraction and resist peeling significantly better than film-forming products. They are also more forgiving on maintenance — recoating does not require stripping the old finish first, which reduces both cost and labor on future applications.
